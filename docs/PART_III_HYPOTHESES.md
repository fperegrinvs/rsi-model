# Part III — Strategy Interpretation: Nested-Hypothesis Assessment

**Status: document work (Third Edition Part III, r3.3 alignment).
Interpretation, not measurement.** The canonical statement is the Third
Edition manuscript §III.3 (`Recursive_Industrial_Intelligence_3rd_Ed_r3.3.md`);
this file is the maintained working version. Assessed under the r2.11/r3.2
correction pass and the r3.3 methods pass: hypotheses are **nested**, the
per-item assessments are **ordinal** (multiplying judgmental likelihood
ratios was methodologically indefensible and is withdrawn), and everything
here concerns **P4**. Per the propositions ladder, P3 is decoupled from P4,
and P4 itself splits: **P4a** (deliberate integrated space–AI–industrial
development) is substantially documented by the S-1's own initiative list;
**P4b** (recursive closure as the objective) remains inferential.

## The nested hypotheses

- **H0** — no *integrated* off-world strategy: individual terrestrial or
  orbital projects arise from ordinary standalone commercial, supply-chain,
  or research motives. (**H0′**: partly related businesses under overlapping
  control — the weakest variant.) Standalone orbital-compute ventures are
  H0-compatible; what H0 does not predict is cross-layer integration toward
  off-world industry.
- **H1** — deliberate integrated space–AI–industrial development (= P4a).
- **H2** — H1 *plus* an intended transition to recursive industrial closure
  (= P4b).

Because the hypotheses nest, each item is assessed on two margins — H1 vs
H0, and H2 vs H1 — with ordinal ratings (*neutral / weak / moderate /
strong*), not numbers: items are correlated (several derive from the same
corporate communications), absence-items depend on assumed observability,
and declared intent is cheap. A biosphere-protection telos (Marshall/Bezos;
[S34]) supplies an additional non-H2 motive for any orbital-compute
observation and caps its diagnosticity for closure intent.

## What the models add to interpretation

1. **Part I names the rational closure move.** The most frequently
   observed minimum cut in the sampled ensemble is the electronics import
   line (fatal in ~9% of otherwise-viable
   central lunar draws and ~75% on Mars; in LEO no single import line was
   fatal in sampled draws — recycling substitutes, making the recycling
   *sector* the dependency instead; n = 40 per cell, wide intervals). A
   closure-seeking actor (H2) would visibly invest there first:
   rad-tolerant lines, stockpile logistics, shelf-life-rated spares,
   off-world packaging/test.
2. **Part II makes orbital-compute persistence a test.** The bridge
   economics are negative at anchored values for the three reference
   architectures (0 of 2,000 sampled runs favor space in each row). The
   Starmind AI1 disclosure scenario narrows the gap but produces only 1 of
   2,000 favorable draws against Earth base and none against responsive
   Earth. A deterministic best case using SpaceX's $185/kg and 100 kW/t
   aspirations plus favorable *unpublished* qualification, life, and
   utilization is 0.76× Earth; the company targets alone are still 1.85×.
   Sustained scaled deployment after the unit economics are publicly evident
   is a moderate-to-strong discriminator for H1/H2 over H0; quiet retreat
   after the demonstration phase is what H0/H0′ predict.
3. **Recycling is the H2 tell in LEO.** Materials-recovery recycling at
   scale (not debris-compliance servicing) is the rational early move of a
   LEO-first closure strategy and of no one else — moderate-to-strong for
   H2 specifically.

## Cumulative assessment (ordinal synthesis, not multiplication)

The evidence **substantially establishes H1** at the level of declared
direction and early implementation: the S-1's initiative list (E6) and the
first-party messaging (E14) are difficult to explain under a purely
terrestrial reading, with E7/E9/E12/E13 as consistent implementation
steps. It **does not establish H2**: no item is more than weakly
diagnostic for closure intent; the closure-specific observables (seed
BOMs, reproduction metrics, minimum-cut roadmaps, locally reproducible
production equipment) remain absent (E10). P4a documented; P4b
inferential.

## Discriminating indicators, model-ranked (ordinal)

1. Production/fabrication equipment explicitly designed for
   extraterrestrial reproduction — **strong for H2**.
2. A cross-company closure program with published κ-vector metrics —
   **strong for H2** (flips E10).
3. A published off-world industrial-seed bill of materials — **strong for
   H2**.
4. Sustained scaled orbital-compute deployment despite publicly evident
   unfavorable unit economics — **moderate-to-strong for H1/H2 vs H0**.
5. Electronics vitamin-gap engineering (stockpile facilities,
   shelf-life-rated spares logistics, off-world packaging/test) —
   **moderate-to-strong for H2** (Part I's minimum cut).
6. On-orbit materials-recovery recycling at scale — **moderate-to-strong
   for H2** (Part I's LEO indispensability result).
7. An export-control-scoped internal model variant (observable: its
   compliance apparatus) — **moderate**.
8. Terafab definitive agreements with committed capital — **weak-to-
   moderate** (upgrades E7; H0 hedging still predicts it).

Threshold observations that would move Appendix A of the manuscript: a
demonstrated ISRU plant above ~50% full-system materials closure; audited
robot production figures; cohort-resolved satellite-failure statistics
with defined denominators; any traceable datum on off-world-producible
electronics tiers — the vitamin-gap anchor, the single most valuable
missing measurement. The thesis *weakens* if humanoid robotics remains
economically narrow, advanced manufacturing cannot be localized, launch
costs stop declining, closure requires a permanently growing Earth supply
chain, verification costs rise faster than capacity — or if Terafab
devolves into design-and-packaging dependent on external front-end
fabrication.
